    How it works

    The idea comes from pro analytics, where it is called ghosting. Coaches use it to ask whether a goal was bad luck or bad positioning.

    Watch

    The model studied thousands of moments from three pro matches. For each one it saw where the ball and every player was, 25 times a second.

    Hide and guess

    In training we hide a defender and ask: he stood here a few seconds ago, the play went like this, where is he now? The model guesses. We show it the truth. Repeat many times.

    Compare

    On a new goal, the guess is the ghost. A defender far from his ghost did something an average pro would not have done. That is where to look first.

    Does it work?

    What it cannot do yet

    Read these before trusting a ghost.

    • It learned from three matches. That is very little. Clubs train this kind of model on hundreds. More data would make the ghosts sharper.
    • A ghost is the average pro, not the perfect one. Far from the ghost means unusual. It does not always mean wrong. A press or an offside trap looks unusual too.
    • It was trained on open play. Corners and free kicks are a different game. The page marks goals that start from a corner.
    • It does not find players in video. The goals here were tracked from TV by someone else. My own camera stage is tested with a pretend camera, not real footage.
    • The TV tracking is rough. Its makers say it was built for animations. Small distances in those clips mean little.
    • The danger score is simple. It only asks who reaches each spot first. It knows nothing about the ball, passing lanes or who the striker is.
